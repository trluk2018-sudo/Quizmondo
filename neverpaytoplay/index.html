A position, not a promotion

Games should not be built to take money from the people least able to see it coming.

Somewhere along the way, playing a game stopped being something you did and became something you bought, repeatedly, in small amounts, for ever. Meanwhile gambling learned to dress itself as a game — levels, streaks, daily missions, chests to open. Never Pay To Play is the other way round.

What we are actually objecting to

Not paying for things. Paying for a game is fine — it always was. A developer makes something, you buy it, it is yours. What replaced that is the problem.

🎁

Paid randomness

A loot box is a bet. You pay, you do not know what you get, and the thing you wanted is rare on purpose.

💳

Pay to win

A game where money substitutes for skill is a leaderboard of who spent most, wearing a game as a costume.

⏳

Manufactured urgency

Limited time, ends tonight, gone for ever. Pressure tactics that would be called out on a shop floor pass without comment from a cartoon.

🚫

Gambling, everywhere

Free spins and welcome offers, served beside things made for ten-year-olds and normalised by sheer repetition.

And it runs the other way too

Gambling learned to look like a game — and it was the faster student

Open a betting app now and it does not look like a bookmaker. It looks like a game. There is a level and a progress bar. Daily missions. A streak you lose if you do not come back tomorrow. Tokens, a wheel to spin, a mystery bonus to reveal, a tier to climb. The language is achievements and rewards; the visual grammar is lifted wholesale from mobile gaming.

It blurs the line until there is no line. A child who has spent three years opening chests recognises every one of those mechanics on sight — and recognises none of them as betting. The skills transfer. The warning does not.

It is one thing to design a game that takes money from adults who can see it coming. It is another to spend a decade teaching children the interface language of a gambling product, and then put a gambling product behind it.

Coins, and which way they point

Earn them. You cannot buy them.

Quizmondo has coins, and that deserves explaining, because a currency is exactly what this page has been objecting to. Here is the difference, and it is the whole difference: you earn coins by playing, and there is no way to buy them. No top-up, no bundle, no "best value" pack. The only way to hold a hundred coins is to have played well enough to be given a hundred.

And they will be worth something. When our store opens, coins will be redeemable against real items in it, and against credit with partner sites we work with — not another cosmetic to show off inside the game that handed it to you.

For brands and agencies

Your ad cannot land next to a betting offer, because there are none to land next to

Brand safety on most inventory means a blocklist and a hope. Here it means something simpler: the things you are trying to avoid are not sold on this site at any price.

Reviewed by a person

Every ad is checked by a human who has seen the creative and the page it leads to, and signed it off as suitable for all ages. Not a filter. Not a network category list.

Nobody is profiled

Advertising here is not personalised. We do not build a picture of a player to sell against. The ICO's Children's Code expects that off by default for children; ours is off for everyone.

An impression means seen

We count an ad when it has genuinely been on screen, not when the page was sent. A smaller number than the industry norm, and the true one.

Attention, not just reach

Someone answering quiz questions is awake and engaged, not thumbing past. That is rarer than impressions.

Formats that behave

Banners, in-feed placements, hero slides and video that never autoplays with sound. Nothing interrupts a round in progress.

Reward-loop placements

Players earn coins that will be redeemable in our store and with partner sites. If you would rather reach people through what they can win, there is a conversation to have.
